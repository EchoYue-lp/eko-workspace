## Claim

Handoff and topology do not introduce a parallel identity, routing, ownership, or
lifecycle authority that overlaps the Subagent model, and no legacy `worker`
terminology exists in either module.

## Method

Repository-wide grep over `echo-agent` and `echo-agent-cli` (all `*.rs` plus
docs/README), executed 2026-08-12:

```text
1. grep -rln -i "handoff" echo-agent/src echo-agent-cli/src echo-agent-cli/echo-agent-app-core/src echo-agent-cli/echo-agent-eval/src
2. grep -rln -i "topology" (same roots)
3. grep -rn -i "worker" echo-agent/src/handoff echo-agent/src/topology.rs
4. grep -rn "SubAgent\b" echo-agent/src/handoff echo-agent/src/topology.rs
5. grep -rn "HandoffManager|HandoffTool|TopologyTracker|TopologyCallback" echo-agent echo-agent-cli --include="*.rs" (excluding defining modules)
6. grep -n "handoff|topology" echo-agent/Cargo.toml (feature graph)
7. grep -n "echo-agent = " echo-agent-cli/Cargo.toml echo-agent-cli/echo-agent-app-core/Cargo.toml (EKO feature enablement)
8. grep -rn "record_call_with_duration" echo-agent/src (producers)
9. grep -rn "CancellationToken" echo-agent/src/handoff echo-agent/src/topology.rs
10. grep -rn "SubAgentMap" echo-agent/src (alias usage)
```

## Conclusion

The claim is falsified in part: handoff introduces a parallel identity/routing
authority (its own registry, its own LLM tool) and a detached lifecycle with zero
cancellation, all unreachable from EKO and only exercised by examples and unit
tests. Topology is a passive observer that cannot see agent-to-agent calls.
Terminology (worker, SubAgent casing) is clean in both modules. Supports findings
F-MAG-01-P1-01, F-MAG-01-P1-02, F-MAG-01-P2-01, F-MAG-01-P2-02. Does not prove
runtime behavior — that is covered by V02 (trace) and V04 (tests).
