## Claim

Timeout ownership is uniform for Sync/Fork/Teammate (the
`SubagentExecutor` owns the timeout, source is
`SubagentDefinition.timeout_secs` > `default_timeout_secs`, applied via
`tokio::time::timeout` or a `select!` sleep arm, and on expiry the child
cancel token is fired so the agent future is dropped). **Team mode's
timeout is owned by `TeamAgent::execute_with_usage`, sourced from
`TeamConfig.default_timeout_secs` (ignoring `SubagentDefinition.timeout_secs`),
floored at `.max(60)`, and on expiry the spawned subagent tasks are
detached (JoinHandles dropped, no `abort()`)**.

## Deviations

The `.max(60)` floor in `TeamAgent::execute_with_usage`
(team/mod.rs:345) is an undocumented invariant: a caller that threads
`timeout_secs(10)` through `TeamAgentBuilder` will get 10 stored in
`TeamConfig.default_timeout_secs`, but `execute_with_usage` will run for
60 seconds. This is recorded as part of F-SUB-02-P2-01.

## Conclusion

Supports finding **F-SUB-02-P2-01**: Team timeout ownership is
disconnected from `SubagentDefinition.timeout_secs` and the `.max(60)`
floor silently overrides sub-minute configuration.

Supports finding **F-SUB-02-P1-02**: on team-level timeout, spawned
subagent tasks are detached (JoinHandles dropped without `abort()`). The
`tokio::spawn` tasks in `execute_sub_tasks` outlive the timeout and
continue consuming an LLM/tool budget with no observer. This is a real
resource leak, not merely a "the answer is late" issue.

Supports finding **F-SUB-02-P2-03** (partial): the absence of a
per-subagent timeout in the production team path means one hung member
holds the whole team hostage until the aggregate timeout.

Does not prove: the exact resource cost of a detached team subagent (it
depends on the concrete `Agent` implementation's willingness to
cooperatively yield). But the structural leak is certain: the JoinHandle
is dropped and the task is not polled to completion by anyone.
