## Claim

In Team mode (ManagerSubagent strategy), when one subagent fails, sibling
subagents are NOT cancelled — they run to completion and their results are
collected alongside the failure for synthesis. There is no fail-fast, no
sibling abort, and no per-subagent timeout; the only termination is the
aggregate team timeout. The `TeamCoordinator`'s reassignment/retry logic
and `TeamRunner`'s per-member timeout exist in the codebase but are **not
on the production dispatch path** (zero live callers).

## Conclusion

Supports finding **F-SUB-02-P2-03**: Team mode has no fail-fast or
sibling-cancellation on partial failure. All subagents run to completion
(or until the aggregate timeout), failures are collected as text and fed
to synthesis, and a total failure still returns `Ok(String)`. Combined
with the absence of per-subagent timeouts (V03), a single hung subagent
blocks the entire team until the aggregate timeout, after which the
running tasks are detached (F-SUB-02-P1-02).

Supports finding **F-SUB-02-P3-02**: `TeamRunner` and
`TeamCoordinator`'s reassignment/retry logic are dead in the production
dispatch path. They implement per-member timeouts and failure-driven
reassignment — exactly the behaviours the production
`ManagerSubagentOrchestrator` lacks — but are never invoked.

Supports finding **F-SUB-02-P3-03**: the non-`ManagerSubagent` strategies
(`Pipeline`/`Debate`/`Swarm`) have no production callers, and `Swarm`
silently drops errors.

Does not prove: whether "collect-all-then-synthesize" is the intended team
semantics (it may be — the synthesis prompt explicitly instructs the
manager to "report failed or blocked sub-tasks truthfully"). The finding
is not that collect-all is wrong, but that (a) there is no per-subagent
timeout, (b) failures do not abort the aggregate budget early, and
(c) the synthesis returns `Ok` even on total failure, which
`dispatch_team` maps to `SubagentStatus::Completed` (executor.rs:1095) —
hiding the failure from the parent's status contract.
