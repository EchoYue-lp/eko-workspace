## Claim

Claim identity is deterministic, attempt-scoped, and rejects stale
replays. Concretely:

1. `TaskClaim` identity is the triple `(revision, attempt, spec_hash)`,
   computed inside `claim_task` under the per-run lock from the
   persisted task state — `revision` is the kernel-supplied
   `expected_revision`, `attempt` is `task.retry_count + 1`, and
   `spec_hash` is the SHA-256 over the canonical `TaskSpec` JSON.
2. The derived `execution_id =
   {run_id}:{task_id}:{revision}:{attempt}` is therefore deterministic
   in the claim alone; the same claim always yields the same id, and
   any change to revision or attempt yields a different id.
3. A stale claim cannot drive a state mutation after: (a) a revision
   that removed or rewrote the task, (b) a cancel/skip that
   transitioned the task out of `Running`, (c) a retry that bumped
   `attempt`, or (d) a crash recovery that reset the task to
   `Pending`/`Blocked` with `claim = null`. In every case,
   `set_claimed_task_status` and `requeue_claimed_task` return
   `Superseded` without appending any event.
4. There is exactly one production construction site for `TaskClaim`
   in `echo-agent-cli` (inside `claim_task`); zero parallel claim
   authorities exist.

## Conclusion

Supports: the "claim identity persistence" invariant. `TaskClaim` is
the single durable lease for one concrete task dispatch attempt; its
identity is deterministic in `(revision, attempt, spec_hash)` and
cannot be replayed after any of those fields changes. The store
enforces this by re-validating the claim on every status write
(`set_claimed_task_status`, `requeue_claimed_task`) and on every
read-side reuse (`task_claim_is_current`,
`recoverable_subagent_result`).

Supports: the "no second claim authority" invariant — there is one
production construction site and one execution_id derivation.

Does NOT support (and is not asked to support): that the naked
`set_task_status` (the non-claim-guarded writer) cannot regress a
terminal task. That is filed as A-TSK-04-P3-01 (defense-in-depth
gap). The claim-guarded writers audited here are sound.

Findings using this validation: none directly (this is the
foundational identity invariant). The findings A-TSK-04-P3-01 and
A-TSK-04-P3-02 are about writers/sweeps that bypass the claim guard,
not about the claim identity itself.
