## Claim

The four delegated claim/recovery findings follow from production framework and
EKO paths, preserve one DAG authority, and are distinct from A-TSK-03 and
F-TSK-03 findings.

## Evidence Sampled

- `echo-agent/echo-orchestration/src/tasks/runtime_executor.rs`: the controller
  loads a revisioned snapshot but calls `block_task(run_id, task, reason)` with
  no expected revision or claim identity.
- `echo-agent-cli/echo-agent-app-core/src/tasks/task_runtime/executor.rs` and
  `store.rs`: the adapter implements that callback with unrestricted
  `set_task_status`, whose only identity check is current task-ID existence.
- `store.rs::recover_incomplete`: selection includes only Running runs, persists
  Paused before per-task repair, and logs/continues repair failures. A second
  invocation therefore cannot select a half-repaired Paused run.
- `store.rs::retry_blocked_task` is the guarded retry authority, while recovery
  resolution uses a plain status mutation and therefore omits budget/attempt
  advancement.
- `file_shadow.rs`, `store.rs`, and `hook_event_dispatcher.rs`: durable append is
  followed by an optional live bounded dispatcher; attachment has neither a
  persisted consumer cursor nor replay of earlier event sequence numbers.

## Result

All four findings are accepted. P1-01 is a missing framework callback fence
plus a thin-adapter violation; P1-02/P1-03 are EKO recovery-policy defects;
P2-01 is a declared lifecycle delivery gap. Existing A-TSK-03 settlement races
and F-TSK-03 claim ABA/abort defects remain backlinks rather than duplicates.
